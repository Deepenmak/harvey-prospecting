## 1. What Optywise sells

Optywise is a Forward Deployment Engineering (FDE) firm. A small pod of senior AI/ML engineers embeds in the client's team and takes one stuck AI pilot or manual workflow to production in six weeks, using the PRISM cycle (Probe, Right-size, Integrate, Secure, Mobilise). The work runs on the client's own cloud (AWS, Azure or GCP), and the client owns the code. Each engagement is a fixed-scope SOW for one use case, priced by outcome, typically $60-150K.

What it can build: multi-agent systems, voice and chat agents, MCP servers, RAG and knowledge grounding, model selection and distillation, document and vision intelligence, intelligent automation, LLM features inside existing products.

Proof on the site:
1. Patient onboarding cut from 45 to 8 minutes (NVelUp)
2. Healthcare voice agents with about 25% fewer no-shows
3. 59% cross-sell lift across five SaaS products (TOSM)
4. 80-90% RFP coverage from a RAG proposal engine (Sectona)
5. Self-healing Playwright QA and vision spec-sheet cleanup (Syndigo)
6. Perlucem banking BI live in production
7. 247% conversion lift from the VartaOS WhatsApp agent (Optywise's own product)
8. Insurance submission intake from hours to minutes (no named client)

The site says it serves "the United States and Canada". The team is in Pune, India, with a +1 512 phone number.

## 2. The buyer in one sentence

A company with 200 to 5,000 employees, in a regulated or document-heavy industry, that has an AI mandate and budget but a pilot stuck at security review, legacy integration or messy data, and no senior AI engineers free to ship it.

Each of these must be true for a high-value prospect:
- **Budget:** $60-150K for one use case is a normal spend, roughly $50M-$2B revenue, or a funded startup.
- **Pain:** a high-volume manual or document workflow (claims, submissions, prior auth, denials, reconciliation, AML alerts, intake, RFPs, support).
- **Intent:** visible AI activity, such as hiring AI/ML roles, a new CIO, CDO or Head of AI, AI in press releases or earnings calls, or a recent funding round.
- **Gap:** no large in-house ML team. Fewer than about 20 ML engineers is the sweet spot.

## 6. Disqualifiers

- Under 100 employees, unless it is a funded AI startup.
- Fortune 500 central IT. Approach a business unit instead, or skip, because procurement and Big 4 incumbents run 6-month cycles.
- Companies with large in-house AI teams (50+ ML engineers).
- IT services and consulting firms (competitors).
- Government and public sector.
- Consumer-only businesses with no regulated or document-heavy workflow.

Looks good on paper but rarely closes. These are starting rules, so check them against the skip reasons after each run:

- Companies whose core product is AI or automation for the workflow we would fix, even if they also run it as a service: they build it themselves. Outsourcers, TPAs and billing firms that run the workflow for clients without selling their own AI for it stay in.
- Subsidiaries whose technology decisions sit with the parent group. Mark them `b` when the parent's role is unclear.
- Companies being acquired, in distress or under a spending freeze.
- A contact whose remit is outside the segment's market.

### Reading the buying state

AI news alone doesn't make a buyer. It can just as easily mean the problem is solved. Judge the workflow we would fix, using these states:

| State | Means | Intent allowed |
|---|---|---|
| `open` | The need is visible and nothing covers it: a stalled or slow pilot, a modernization program with no vendor named for this workflow, a current hire for it, a new AI or digital leader | high, medium or low |
| `partly_solved` | A vendor or in-house tool covers this workflow, but another one in the same business is still open | medium at most |
| `solved` | A vendor or in-house system is live across the workflow, and nothing else open was found | low |
| `builds_own` | Their core product is AI for this workflow, or they run a large in-house AI team | low |
| `unknown` | No evidence either way | high, medium or low |

Strongest signals first, when choosing what to lead with: a stalled pilot or a failed vendor project, a current AI or automation hire for the workflow, a new AI or digital leader in the last 12 months, a dated modernization program, then a regulatory deadline, M&A, funding or fast growth.

An account is tier `A`, worth 2-3 contacts, when fit is `a`, intent is `high`, the state is `open` and the facts passed checking. Harvey sets this itself, and it never spends credits by itself.

## 8. How to source them

Local business listings are the wrong tool for this ICP. Use these:
1. **Apollo:** search by industry, employee count, country and title, once the key is in .env.
2. **Harvey's Serper presets:** hiring (careers pages listing the roles above) and funding (companies announcing a raise).
3. **Harvey's job-board discovery** (python-jobspy) for companies hiring AI roles.
4. **Harvey's profile step** on every company found, to add hiring and stack signals, then the cohort builder to rank.

Start with one segment and one market: US Tier 1 insurance (MGAs and mid-size carriers), about 50 companies, before widening.
